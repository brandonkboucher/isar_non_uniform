\begin{abstract}
Inverse Synthetic Aperture Radar (ISAR) enables imaging in any weather, day or night, but relies on the motion of a non-cooperative target to form an image. The crossrange resolution is dependent on angular diversity and is susceptible to aliasing if the pulse repetition frequency (PRF) is insufficient to process the incoming Doppler bandwidth of the received echoes. Doppler aliasing injects ghost scatterers into the unambiguous Doppler region which can be difficult to differentiate from authentic scatterers. Our proposed solution, Doppler Ambiguity Resolving Orthogonal Matching Pursuit (DAR-OMP), is a variant of OMP capable of disambiguating target scatterers through the target's non-uniform motion. DAR-OMP identifies aliased targets in the unambiguous region and determines the originating ambiguity by maximizing the correlation of the residual with a localized neighborhood of candidate, ambiguous atoms. Additionally, DAR-OMP handles off-grid scatterers by applying Newton’s method with backtracking to refine the off-grid positions of the most probable candidate atoms. DAR-OMP is computationally efficient and scales easily by processing the latent image in a singular ambiguity. Preliminary results demonstrate as much as an 87.4\% reduction in root mean squared error (RMSE) in comparison to Newtonized OMP with an expanded latent image containing the additional Doppler ambiguities. DAR-OMP is a light-weight, fast OMP variant capable of disambiguating target scatterers from different Doppler ambiguities by leveraging the non-uniform motion of a non-cooperative target.
\end{abstract}
